Mod Alpha — What It Costs To Run

A simple breakdown of monthly costs at 10,000 / 50,000 / 100,000 and 1 million paying customers

This covers a simpler version of Mod Alpha: we tell people the best day each month to invest in a mutual fund — we don't place trades for them, and we don't read their bank or investment account data. Customers pick the funds they're interested in themselves, verify their identity (ID check) to subscribe, and we send them a notification when it's a good day to invest.

1. What Changed, And Why It's Cheaper Now

We simplified the product in two ways, and that made it much cheaper and much simpler to run.

❌ What we removed

  • Automatically reading a customer's bank/investment account data
  • Connecting to stockbrokers to place trades (Zerodha, Groww, Upstox, Angel One)
  • The system that would place and track trades on someone's behalf
  • Special network setup that's only needed for placing trades
  • A complex trade-logging system (replaced with a simpler one)
  • Checking for accidental double-investments (not needed — we never see their trades)
  • ID verification for people who are just browsing, not paying

✅ What we still need

  • ID verification (KYC) for anyone who pays to subscribe
  • The AI that picks the best investment date — now worked out once per fund, not separately for every customer
  • Sending notifications by app alert, text message, and email
  • Collecting subscription payments
  • The mobile app and the website
  • Security testing, keeping records of what we told customers, and following India's data privacy law
Why this matters The two things we removed — reading people's investment accounts automatically, and connecting to brokers to place trades — were the most expensive parts of the original plan (up to ₹95,000 a month just for account-reading fees, at 50,000 customers). Without them, Mod Alpha looks much more like a simple subscription app than a trading platform — and it's cheaper, simpler, and lower-risk to run.
What's not included here Getting registered as a licensed financial advisor with SEBI (India's market regulator) is being handled as a separate piece of work, so that cost is not included in the numbers below. This document only covers what it costs to run the servers, verify IDs, and send notifications.

The headline result Running Mod Alpha now looks like running a simple subscription app, not an expensive trading platform. It costs about ₹23 to serve one paying customer for a month when we have 10,000 customers — and that drops to about ₹10 once we reach 1 million, because the big shared costs (servers, AI engine) get spread across more people, and bulk pricing on ID-checks and notifications kicks in. The share of revenue left over as profit improves from 87% to 94% over that same growth.

7. What Could Change These Numbers

What we could changeWhat it does to cost
How much we rely on text messages vs just app alertsThis is the single biggest cost we actually control. Relying mostly on app alerts instead of text messages could cut notification costs by roughly 60%
Negotiating cheaper ID-verification pricing as we growGoing from ₹65 to ₹50 per person once we're verifying a million people saves about ₹15 per person
How often the AI recalculates the best investment datesBarely matters — running it daily instead of weekly costs almost nothing extra, since it's worked out per fund, not per customer
What share of app users actually pay to subscribeDoesn't change our server costs directly, since ID-checks and notifications only apply to paying customers — but it does matter for how many "free" users we're supporting without any revenue from them
Negotiating a lower payment processing feeWorth doing once we're large: even a quarter-percent reduction saves a meaningful amount of money at 1 million customers
What's not included in this model Getting licensed as a financial advisor with SEBI, legal reviews specific to giving investment advice, money spent on marketing to get new customers, and salaries — none of that is included here. This covers only servers, ID verification, and sending notifications.

What this tells us Even in the worst case we tested (mostly text messages + customers paying only ₹99/month), we'd still keep 75–80% of revenue as profit at both sizes. The business holds up well under these swings because our two "must-pay" costs — ID verification and servers — are already small. The biggest thing we can control is how much we rely on text messages, not pricing: switching from mostly-texts to mostly-app-alerts roughly doubles our profit cushion (e.g., at 100,000 customers paying ₹180/month: 85.3% → 94.4%).
One reality check App notifications don't always arrive reliably in India — many Android phone makers (like Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo) aggressively shut down background apps to save battery, which can block notifications from getting through. So "mostly app alerts" is our best-case, cheapest scenario — in practice, we may need to lean on some text messages whether we want to or not. Treat the "Mixed" numbers as the realistic plan, and "mostly app alerts" as the best case if everything goes smoothly.
